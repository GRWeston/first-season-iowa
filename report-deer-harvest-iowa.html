Iowa Deer Harvest Reporting: How to Tag and Report Your Deer

You got your deer. Before the excitement wears off, there are a few things Iowa requires you to do, in a specific order. Here’s the deer harvest report process, step by step.

By Renee Okafor, who nervously reported her first deer from the tailgate of a borrowed truck.

Tag, report, then process

  1. Confirm the deer is down

    Approach carefully from behind and watch for movement. Give the animal time. Make sure you’re safe before you set your weapon down.

  2. Attach your tag before moving the deer

    Fill out your transportation tag and attach it to the deer, usually to a leg or antler, before you drag it anywhere. This is the step new hunters most often forget.

  3. Report the harvest to the DNR

    Iowa requires a deer harvest report for every deer, filed by the deadline, which has been midnight on the day after harvest. You can report online, through the state’s mobile app, or by phone. You’ll be asked for your tag number, the county, and whether the deer is antlered or antlerless.

  4. Write down your confirmation number

    When you finish reporting, you’ll get a confirmation or registration number. Write it on your tag. That number shows the deer was legally reported.

  5. Field dress and cool the meat

    Cool the carcass quickly, especially on mild days. If you’re taking it to a locker for processing, call ahead during the busy gun seasons.

What happens if you forget to report? Failing to report a deer is a violation of Iowa law and can mean a fine. If you miss the deadline, report it as soon as you remember and contact the DNR for guidance. Don’t just skip it.

How to register a deer in Iowa: a common question

“Registering” and “reporting” usually mean the same thing in Iowa. There’s no separate check station visit. Reporting your harvest online, in the app, or by phone is how the DNR records it.

Donating venison

If you can’t use all of the meat, Iowa has a program that lets hunters donate deer to participating lockers to feed families in need. Ask your local locker whether it participates.
