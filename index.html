Iowa Deer Hunting for Beginners: How to Start

Didn’t grow up hunting? Neither did a lot of people sitting in a tree stand this fall. This guide takes you from zero to your first season of hunting deer in Iowa in five steps, in the order you’ll actually do them.

By Walt Kessler, who has hunted southeast Iowa for 22 seasons and has taken six first-time adults on their first hunt.

Your path to a first deer hunt

  1. Take hunter education

    Most new hunters in Iowa need to finish a hunter education course before they can buy a regular license. It covers firearm safety, ethics, and Iowa law. If you want to try hunting first, an apprentice license may let you hunt with a mentor while you decide.

    How to get an Iowa deer hunting license

  2. Buy your license and deer tag

    You’ll need a hunting license, the habitat fee, and a deer tag that matches the season and weapon you plan to use. You can buy them online, in a phone app, or at many sporting goods stores.

  3. Pick a season and gather your gear

    Iowa has archery, muzzleloader, and shotgun seasons. Your season decides your weapon, and your weapon decides most of your gear list. Blaze orange is required during the firearm seasons.

    See the beginner deer hunting gear list

  4. Find a place to hunt

    Ask farmers for permission early, and well before season opens. If you don’t know any landowners, Iowa has public wildlife areas and walk-in hunting land open to licensed hunters.

  5. Know what to do after the shot

    Before you move your deer, tag it. Then report the harvest to the DNR by the deadline. It takes about five minutes, and skipping it can cost you a fine.

    Iowa deer harvest reporting, step by step

Find a mentor if you can. One season sitting next to an experienced hunter will teach you more than any website, including this one. Ask coworkers, classmates, or the staff at a local sporting goods store whether they know anyone willing to take a beginner along.

Why this guide exists

The official rules are on the DNR website, and hunting tips are spread across forums written for people who already hunt. Nobody puts the whole process in order for a beginner. We wrote First Season Iowa to be the guide we wish someone had handed us: plain language, one step at a time, and a reminder to check the official rules wherever details matter.
