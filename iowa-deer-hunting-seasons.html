Iowa Deer Hunting Seasons Explained for Beginners

Iowa doesn’t have one deer season. It has several, spread from September to January, and each one decides which weapon you can carry. Here’s how the Iowa deer hunting season calendar fits together, and which season makes the most sense for your first year.

By Renee Okafor, who spent her first season in a ground blind during the second shotgun season.

How the seasons are organized

Iowa splits deer hunting by weapon and by time of year. Each season has its own license and tag, so the season you choose is really the first decision you make, before you buy anything. The exact dates change every year and are set by the Iowa Department of Natural Resources, so treat the descriptions below as a general map and confirm this year’s dates with the DNR.

Each Iowa deer hunting season, in order

  1. Youth season

    A special season for resident youth, usually running in the second half of September. Young hunters go with an adult mentor, and it’s the earliest chance in the year to be in the field.

  2. Early muzzleloader season

    A short season in October for hunters using muzzleloading rifles. Deer are still on early-fall patterns, and the woods are quieter than they will be later.

  3. Archery season

    The longest season. It opens in the fall, pauses for the shotgun seasons, then reopens and runs into January. Bowhunting takes practice, but the long window gives you many chances to learn.

  4. First and second shotgun seasons

    Two short seasons in December. Most of Iowa’s deer are harvested during these weeks, and many beginners start here because group hunts and mentors are easiest to find. Blaze orange is required.

  5. Late muzzleloader season

    Follows the second shotgun season and runs into January. Cold weather and deer that have already been pressured make it a challenging but rewarding hunt.

Which season should a beginner choose?

For most adults starting out, one of the December shotgun seasons is the easiest first step. The seasons are short, more hunters are out, and it’s the most likely time to find someone willing to bring you along. If you’d rather hunt alone and on your own schedule, archery season gives you months instead of days, but plan on practicing with a bow all summer before you go.

Get this year’s exact dates from the DNR. Season dates shift slightly every year with the calendar. Check the Iowa DNR’s current hunting regulations before you buy a license, and put opening day in your phone the day you buy your tag.
