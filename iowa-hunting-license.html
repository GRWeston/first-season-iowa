Iowa Hunting License and the Apprentice Option

Before you can hunt deer in Iowa, you need two things sorted out: hunter education and the right license. Here’s how each works, and how the apprentice license lets you try hunting before you finish the course.

By Renee Okafor, who took up hunting at 29 and finished hunter education the same year.

Do I need hunter education?

If you were born after January 1, 1972, Iowa requires you to complete hunter education before you can buy a regular hunting license. The course teaches safe firearm handling, shot placement, hunting ethics, and the basics of Iowa hunting law.

How the course works

Most people take the course online at their own pace and then attend an in-person field day, where an instructor checks that you can handle a firearm safely. Field days fill up quickly in late summer and early fall, so sign up early if you want to hunt this season.

What the Iowa apprentice hunting license is

The apprentice license is for people who want to try hunting before committing to the full course. It lets you hunt without hunter education for a limited number of license years, as long as you stay with a licensed adult mentor who meets the DNR’s requirements.

Who it’s good for

  • Adults who aren’t sure yet whether hunting is for them
  • People invited on a hunt this fall who can’t fit in a field day first
  • Anyone who wants to learn alongside a mentor before going alone

The apprentice license has limits. You can’t hunt alone with it, and you can only use it for a set number of years. Eventually you’ll need to finish hunter education to keep hunting. Check the current rules on the Iowa DNR site for the exact age, mentor, and year requirements.

How to get a hunting license in Iowa

Once you’ve finished hunter education, or decided on the apprentice route, you can buy a license online, through the state’s mobile app, or at a license agent like a sporting goods store. To hunt deer, you’ll generally need:

  • A hunting license
  • The wildlife habitat fee
  • A deer tag for the season and weapon you plan to use

Keep your license and tag with you whenever you hunt. A screenshot isn’t always enough, so learn how your tag needs to be carried and attached before opening day.
